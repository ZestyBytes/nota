Not a fern at all, but a relative of the edible asparagus, which is why it grows in long arching sprays rather than fronds. This one has clearly decided that whatever it is standing on belongs to it. The needles are soft, the stems less so: there are small thorns hidden along them.

## Care

- The thirstiest of the lot. Keep it evenly moist and it stays green; let it dry out and it drops needles everywhere.
- Yellowing sprays usually mean too little water or too much direct sun.
- Cut old or bare stems right down at the base. New ones come up from the crown.
- It grows from fat water storing tubers, so it will forgive one missed week but not a fortnight.
- Mist it, or give it a shower under the tap now and then, which also sees off any red spider mite.
- Wear gloves when you cut it back, and keep it away from anything that grazes on leaves.
- In a windowless room it grows sparser and reaches for what light there is. Keep it in the spill from the glazed door and turn it weekly.
