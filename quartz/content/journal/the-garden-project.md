![[garden-before-hammock.jpeg|August 2024: the lawn worn through to soil, and a hammock nobody used]]

For two summers this was a garden the children ran across rather than one
anyone sat in. The lawn was worn down to soil wherever people actually
walked, the fences were going quietly brown, and watering it made very
little difference.

![[garden-before-sprinkler.jpeg|The sprinkler on, thirteen days later, to about the same effect]]

So this spring we took the whole thing out and started again.

![[IMG_3985.jpeg|The plan, marked up in red]]

Dug out, rotavated and levelled. A new lawn laid. Two planters built, one
along the patio and one running down the far edge. The fences cleared and
painted. Then the planting, which is the point where it stops looking like a
building site and starts looking like a garden.

![[IMG_4035.jpeg|30 May: turf down, the first planter built, sprinkler running]]

![[IMG_4045.jpeg|31 May: planted up, laurels going in along the fence]]

Start to finish it took a week.

![[IMG_4051.jpeg|1 June, ten at night, with the lights up the tree]]

The last photograph is the one that made it worth the effort. Ten at night,
lights up the tree, and somewhere to sit that we would not have bothered
walking out to a month earlier.

What I want from it next is smaller and slower: herbs close enough to the
kitchen to be worth using, tomatoes that taste of summer, somewhere the
children can dig without it mattering, and enough notes to remember what
worked when spring comes round again.
