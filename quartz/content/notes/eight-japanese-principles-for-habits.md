Eight ideas for starting and keeping habits, saved from a carousel post and
rewritten in my own words. The through-line is that none of them ask for
more motivation — they lower the cost of starting, or make stopping the
harder option.

**1. Kaizen — the one-minute rule.** Start with something so small the brain
can't object: one push-up, one sentence, one minute of the thing.

**2. Ikigai — a reason to get up.** The question isn't what you do, it's why
you get up. A clear "why" makes the effort cheaper.

**3. Hara hachi bu — stop at 80% full.** Eating past comfortable costs focus
and energy. Leaving a little room keeps the afternoon usable.

**4. Anchor the focus — build a ritual.** Twenty-five minutes on, five off,
opened by the same small cue each time — a breath, a phrase, a gesture.
Repetition teaches the brain what's about to happen.

**5. Seiri and seiton — sort, then set in order.** Clutter is mental noise.
A tidied space isn't vanity, it's fewer decisions before starting.

**6. Kintsugi — finish it even when it's flawed.** Procrastination is often
fear of a bad result. Finishing builds momentum; waiting for perfect keeps
you still.

**7. Wabi-sabi — act before conditions are right.** Start with what you
have. A lot of procrastination is fear wearing patience as a disguise.

**8. Build a system — track the progress.** Motivation alone doesn't change
behaviour. Visible progress does, and a system outlasts the mood that
started it.

Worth remembering that kintsugi and wabi-sabi are aesthetic philosophies
about repair and impermanence rather than productivity advice; this is the
post's framing of them, not their original meaning.
